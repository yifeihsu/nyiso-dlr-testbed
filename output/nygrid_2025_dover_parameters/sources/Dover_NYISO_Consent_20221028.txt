         On July 22, 2021, New York Transco LLC (“Transco”) notified the NYISO of four
proposed modifications to the New York Energy Solution project (“Transmission Project”) that
the NYISO Board of Directors selected as the more efficient or cost-effective solution to
Segment B of the AC Transmission Public Policy Transmission Need (“Segment B Need”) and
that is currently being developed by Transco pursuant to the Development Agreement Among
the New York Independent System Operator, Inc. and Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a
National Grid and New York Transco, LLC, dated January 10, 2020 (“Development
Agreement”). Transco also notified the NYISO of additional proposed modifications on July 5,
2022, and August 5, 2022. Based on the NYISO’s review, including discussions with Transco,
the NYISO hereby consents to the modifications listed in Transco’s July 22, 2021, July 5, 2022,
and August 5, 2022, correspondence in accordance with Article 3.5 of the Development
Agreement.

        Under Article 3.5 of the Development Agreement, a Developer is only permitted to make
a Significant Modification to a Transmission Project with the prior written consent of the
NYISO. The Development Agreement defines a “Significant Modification” as:

       a Developer’s proposed modification to its Transmission Project that: (i) could
       impair the Transmission Project’s ability to meet the identified Public Policy
       Transmission Need, (ii) could delay the In-Service Date of the Transmission Project
       beyond the Required Project In-Service Date, or (iii) would constitute a material
       change to the project information submitted by the Developer under Attachment Y
       of the OATT for use by the NYISO in evaluating the Transmission Project for
       purposes of selecting the more efficient or cost-effective transmission solution to
       meet the identified Public Policy Transmission Need.

        If a proposed modification meets the definition of a Significant Modification, Article 3.5
of the Development Agreement provides that the NYISO may not unreasonably withhold or
condition its consent as long as the Developer demonstrates to the NYISO’s satisfaction that the
proposed modification (i) does not impair the Transmission Project’s ability to satisfy the
identified Public Policy Transmission Need, (ii) does not delay the In-Service Date of the

       The NYISO has determined that the proposed modifications, as detailed below, satisfy
one or more of the elements of the definition of Significant Modification under the Development
Agreement. As a result, the NYISO reviewed each proposed modification to determine whether
the NYISO could consent under the elements of Article 3.5 of the Development Agreement.

       The proposed modifications in the July 22, 2021 request consist of (i) the change to the
       series compensation system from a one-step (50%) to a two-step (33.3% and 17.67%)
       system, (ii) the Van Wagner Capacitor Bank Station, (iii) the Rock Tavern to Sugarloaf
       rebuild, and (iv) the replacement of equipment at the Coopers Corners and 345 kV Rock
       Tavern Substation to meet the existing conductor rating of the Marcy South path between
       Coopers Corner and the East Transition Station.

       The proposed modification in the July 5, 2022 request consists of the installation of a new
       substation (“Dover PAR Substation”), which includes two 345 kV 450/600/750 MVA
       PARs and associated bus work, breakers, switches and protection and control equipment.
       The NYISO preliminarily identified the need for a similar upgrade to address thermal
       degradation on the New York – New England intertie in the project’s System Impact
       Study. The Dover PAR Substation will mitigate the identified degradation.

       The proposed modification in the July 5, 2022 request helps clarify the July 22, 2021
       request to also remove certain terminal equipment upgrades on the 305 line between
       Central Hudson’s Roseton Substation and Con Edison’s East Fishkill Substation from the
       project.

        The NYISO has determined that the proposed Significant Modifications meet the four
prongs set forth in Article 3.5 of the Development Agreement. Specifically, the modifications
would not impair the Transmission Project’s ability to satisfy the Segment B Need, and as
represented by Transco, would not delay the In-Service Date of the Transmission Project beyond
the Required Project In-Service Date. Moreover, while the proposed modifications result in
material changes to the project information, including costs, submitted under Attachment Y of
the OATT for use by the NYISO in evaluating the Transmission Project, the NYISO is satisfied
that those changes do not affect the grounds upon which it selected the Transmission Project as
the more efficient or cost-effective transmission solution. Furthermore, the NYISO studied the
proposed changes through the NYISO’s Transmission Interconnection Procedures and received
additional analysis related to the Public Policy Transmission Planning Process and did not find
that such changes resulted in any significant adverse impact to the reliability of the New York
State Transmission System. Accordingly, the NYISO consents to the proposed modifications.
