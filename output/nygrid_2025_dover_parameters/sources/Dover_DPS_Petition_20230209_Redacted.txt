III.     The Project

             a. The PAR Station And Interconnection Facilities

         The Project’s primary component is the construction of the new PAR Station. The PAR

Station’s major equipment includes two parallel 750 Mega Volt Ampere (MVA) PARs with circuit

breakers and bypass switches, one A-frame dead-end structure, rigid bus support structures,

miscellaneous yard equipment, a control house building, and a spare PAR. The third PAR will be

stored onsite and left unenergized in case one of the energized units requires replacement. In

addition, minor relay modification work will be performed at the Con Edison Cricket Valley

Energy Center Substation in Dover, New York and the Eversource Long Mountain Substation in

Connecticut.

         The PAR Station will interconnect with Line 39811 between structures L-61 and L-62 in

the Town between the Cricket Valley Energy Center Substation and the Eversource Long

Mountain Substation. Work in the Line 398 ROW to accommodate the PAR Station’s

interconnection and complete the Project will entail replacing structure L-61 and installing a new

structure mid-span between L-61 and L-62 that will cut Line 398 into the PAR Station.12

              b. The PAR Station Site

          The PAR Station will be located on an approximately 16.3-acre parcel in the Town at 2238

district. “Public utility facilities” such as the PAR Station are permitted in the CO zoning district

by special permit issued by the Planning Board.13 Thus, Transco applied for a special use permit

Energy Center, and the Con Edison 345 kV transmission line (Line 398) ROW where the Project

will interconnect. The Iroquois Gas Transmission pipeline is also located adjacent to the Con

Edison ROW in proximity to the Site.

IV.      Argument

                  The Commission Should Grant Transco A CPCN Because The
                   Project Is Convenient And Necessary For The Public Service

         PSL § 68 (1) requires electric corporations to obtain a CPCN from the Commission prior

to beginning construction of an electric plant. The Commission may grant a CPCN after

determining, following a due hearing, that the Project’s construction “is convenient and necessary

for the public service.”25 In making that determination, the Commission must consider: (a) project

need and public interest; (b) the project’s economic feasibility and petitioner’s ability to finance

the improvement; and (c) petitioner’s ability to provide safe, adequate, and reliable service at just

and reasonable rates.26 Further, an applicant seeking a CPCN must satisfy all other applicable

regulatory requirements.27

         In applying these and other requirements of PSL Article 4 to projects developed under

lightened regulation, however, the Commission has recognized that “[a]uthority over these matters

has been exercised flexibly, at our discretion, with the extent of scrutiny afforded a particular

transaction reduced to the level the public interest requires.”28 Because Transco is a lightly

regulated entity, this standard of review applies here.29

        a. Need And Public Interest

        PSL § 68 (1) and 16 NYCRR § 21.3 (f) require that the Commission consider whether

issuing a CPCN is in the public interest. Moreover, 16 NYCRR § 21.3 (g) requires a petitioner to

provide evidence that there is a public need for the proposed project.

        The Project’s need and public interest considerations are well-documented. In Case 12-T-

0502, the Commission identified the AC transmission corridor connecting the Mohawk Valley

Region, the Lower Hudson Valley region, and the Capitol Region as a source of persistent

congestion.30 The Commission ultimately determined that there was a transmission need—the AC

Transmission PPTN—“driven by Public Policy Requirements for new 345 kV major electric

transmission facilities to cross the Central East and UPNY/SENY interfaces to provide additional

transmission capacity to move power from upstate to downstate.”31 The Commission divided the

AC Transmission PPTN into two segments—Segment A (i.e., Central East interface) and Segment

B (i.e., UPNY/SENY interface). After declaring the AC Transmission PPTN, the Commission

ordered the NYISO to solicit solutions to the AC Transmission PPTN.32

        After an extensive study, the NYISO selected Transco’s NYES Project to satisfy Segment

B of the AC Transmission PPTN.33 The NYISO also conducted an SIS to evaluate the impact of

the then-proposed NYES Project on the reliability of the New York State transmission system and

identified the PAR Station as one of the upgrades necessary to ensure that power flows reliably
