# NYgrid generation-source audit — 2026-09-08

No complete, directly observed 11-zone hourly generation series was found in the checked public NYISO products for the six 2019 and six 2025 study hours. The new snapshot input module correctly leaves these observations missing. A separately labeled generation reconstruction is feasible; it is not a substitute for measured zonal dispatch.

## Paper and retrieved evidence

M. Vivienne Liu, Bo Yuan, Zongjie Wang, Jeffrey A. Sward, K. Max Zhang, and C. Lindsay Anderson, **“An Open Source Representation for the NYS Electric Grid to Support Power Grid and Market Transition Studies,” IEEE Transactions on Power Systems, 38(4), 3293–3303, July 2023**, DOI [10.1109/TPWRS.2022.3200887](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRS.2022.3200887). Online publication was 24 August 2022. The [open final paper at the coauthor's UConn site](https://sustainable-power-energy-research.media.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3441/2023/11/An-Open-Source-Representation-for-the-NYS-Electric-Grid-to-Support-Power-Grid-and-Market-Transition-Studies.pdf) was retrieved as `Liu_2023_TPWRS_NYgrid.pdf`; SHA256 `d0c5da107cd38304f9a235862c54ee9cf5147a28a3999008be2214b8d7b29f8a`.

The paper explicitly estimates unavailable generator profiles. It uses 140 RGGI-tracked fossil generators (92.4% of thermal nameplate capacity), compensates for untracked smaller units, assumes daily nuclear availability within each day, and combines monthly hydro information with statewide fuel mix. Wind and other renewables are distributed using capacity shares. Its validation is DC power flow against major interfaces and DC-OPF against prices; it does not establish observed AC voltages or reactive capability. The model retains 57 buses after modifying and reducing the NPCC system. See paper Sections III–V.

The [NYgrid repository](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/tree/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e) was checked at main commit `47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e`, dated 2022-06-01. Selected files and the repository tree were downloaded here. No external code was executed.

## What the source code actually does

- [updateOpCond.m](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/updateOpCond.m#L179): initializes thermal dispatch from the matched hourly records; missing records become zero. The difference from the statewide generation requirement is allocated entirely to active zone-J units in this pinned version (coefficient 1 at line 210), while K receives coefficient 0 at line 218. This is more specific than the paper's J/K description and should not be silently copied as observed zonal behavior. Hydro uses a monthly St. Lawrence factor, assigns the remaining share of 80% statewide hydro to Niagara, and spreads 20% among smaller hydro. Nuclear uses daily data. Wind and other renewables become negative load. The code gives thermal units Q bounds of ±9999; these are unsuitable as evidence of physical AC capability.
- [allocateGen.m](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/Utility/allocateGen.m): joins generator parameters, bus assignments and hourly records by NYISO name and PTID. This is more informative than a generic zonal capacity-share prior, but requires identity and gross/net accounting checks before reuse.
- [allocateLoad.m](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/Utility/allocateLoad.m): allocates actual hourly zonal loads using original within-zone weights; where no original load exists, including G/J, it allocates evenly. It assumes load power factor 0.98.
- [writeInterflow.m](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/Utility/writeInterflow.m#L36) and [writeFuelmix.m](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/Utility/writeFuelmix.m#L36) form hourly means. Our current campaign uses exactly matching published hour labels from hourly load and a P32 point sample, explicitly marking the sampling intervals unequal. The two methods must not be described as identical temporal observations.
- The [external-flow calculation](https://github.com/AndersonEnergyLab-Cornell/NYgrid/blob/47698b6c7823ae7b1bc6935e5601d5b64b8f918e/updateOpCond.m#L248) sums HQ-NY plus Cedars, primary PJM plus HTP/Neptune/VFT, primary NE plus 1385/CSC, and Ontario. It excludes HQ_IMPORT_EXPORT. This independently corroborates our selected ten-channel accounting policy, while the exact algebra of the overlapping HQ report remains unverified.

## Public observation coverage

| Source checked | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [NYISO P63 fuel mix](https://mis.nyiso.com/public/P-63list.htm) | Seven statewide fuel categories at roughly five-minute intervals; archives cover 2019/2025. The cached 2019-07-20 file and freshly downloaded July-2025 ZIP member 2025-07-29 both have only timestamp, timezone, fuel category and Gen MW. | Eleven-zone or individual-unit dispatch. No zone field exists. |
| [NYISO P61 dispatch analysis](https://mis.nyiso.com/public/P-61list.htm) | Aggregate differences between actual and ideal economic dispatch. | Actual zonal/unit MW profiles. The public index inspected contains old chart archives, not the requested modern telemetry. |
| [EPA hourly operations and EPA–EIA crosswalk](https://www.epa.gov/power-sector/power-sector-data-crosswalk) | Hourly combustion-source operations with identity links to EIA generators. | Complete generation from nuclear, hydro, wind and solar; automatic one-record/one-generator identity. EPA explicitly warns of different collection units and matching difficulty. |
| [EIA-923](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia923/) | Monthly/annual plant and prime-mover generation and fuel data. | Coincident hourly generation. The 2025 annual data were labeled early release when checked. |
| [NRC reactor status](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/event-status/reactor-status/2025/index) | Daily reactor power status, with links to 2019/2025 archives. | Direct hourly net electric output at each study instant. |

This bounded search is a statement about the products inspected, not a claim that no restricted, vendor, or separately released dataset could exist.

## Comparison with this repository and recommended use

The inherited `s12_build_priors.m` is a different, simpler construction: it spreads system fuel-mix totals across eligible PERFORM units in proportion to capacity, merges dual-fuel/natural-gas pools, and applies matched winter capacities. Its 2019 zonal CSVs are therefore allocated priors, not public observations. The older `nyiso_zonal_generation_targets.csv` is derived from incomplete internal-interface incidence; some values are negative, reinforcing that it is not a measured dispatch table.

The new compact snapshot module preserves independent raw load/interface/external evidence and leaves generator observations missing. Its default scale is fixed per vintage using the registered summer peak; all powers and interface limits use that same gamma. It retains seasonal load variation and distinguishes the old constant-total shape diagnostic. Generation fitting can use independently constructed priors, but 2019 validation and held-out interface targets must not influence those priors.

A stronger future prior would match EPA/EIA/NYISO plant identities for each vintage, preserve missing/partial coverage, reconcile gross versus net output and time conventions, combine NRC/EIA/fuel-mix information with labeled assumptions, then aggregate to model zones. That would improve a defensible reconstruction without inventing 11-zone observed truth. No such complete plant reconstruction was performed in this audit, and the 2022 NYgrid repository cannot supply a 2025 fleet without updating additions, retirements and identities.
